## DRAFT BEGINS

Last week, Meta launched something. They didn't announce it in your Instagram feed. They didn't send a push notification explaining it. They just turned it on — and your public photos were already included.

It's called Muse Image, and here's what it does: anyone can go to Meta AI, tag your public Instagram account, type a prompt, and generate images that use your photos as source material. Images of your face. Your style. Your life. Composited into things you never did, in places you've never been, alongside people you don't know.

Your account was opted in automatically. Not "opted in unless you say no" as a policy failure — opted in as the intended design. [YOUR TAKE ON THIS]

---

Let me explain the mechanics, because the way this works matters.

Muse Image is an AI image generation tool. The company trained it on images — as all image AI is — and it can generate new pictures based on text descriptions. That much is familiar territory by now.

The difference is that Muse Image was designed with a specific feature: you can point it at any public Instagram account and use that account's photos as a visual reference. The AI reads those images, identifies what makes them distinctive — the faces, the settings, the aesthetic — and incorporates that into whatever it generates.

So if you have a public Instagram and someone types "Maria at the beach, golden hour, summer dress," Muse Image doesn't just make a stock-photo-looking version of that. It makes a version that looks like you, because it used your photos to understand what you look like. SAG-AFTRA told its members to opt out immediately. The Creative Artists Agency said the policy was "completely unacceptable." Privacy advocates who have spent years watching this pattern aren't surprised, but they're alarmed by how direct it is.

What's different about Muse Image compared to earlier AI training controversies is the specificity. Before, the concern was "companies scraped the internet and my work was in there somewhere." That's abstract. It's hard to picture. It's hard to feel.

This is not abstract. Any user, anywhere, can type your Instagram handle and generate images of you. The scale of prior AI training debates — billions of images, millions of artists — is replaced by something much more personal: one person, your face, right now.

Here is what you can do.

Go to your Instagram settings. Look for Privacy, then AI tools. [VERIFY THIS: exact navigation path — confirm it hasn't moved since the feature launched] You should find an option to opt out of Muse Image. Toggle it off.

That's the short version. The longer version is: you have to decide what you actually want.

If you have a public Instagram because you're building an audience, promoting creative work, or just sharing your life, you've already made a choice that your photos are visible to the public. Muse Image changes what "visible" means. Before, people could see your photos. Now, people can use your photos as inputs to generate new images. Those are different things, and you get to have an opinion about whether you wanted the second one.

If you opt out, you're opting out of the feature that lets other users reference your photos. You may also be opting out of some other Meta AI functionality — the settings aren't entirely granular.
