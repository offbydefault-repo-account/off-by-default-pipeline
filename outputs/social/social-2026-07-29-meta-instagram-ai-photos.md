### LinkedIn (~800 characters, max 1,800)

In early July, Meta turned on a feature called Muse Image. If you have a public Instagram account, you were already enrolled before the announcement reached most people.

Here's what the feature does: any user can open Meta AI, type your Instagram handle, and generate new images using your photos as reference material. Not just your aesthetic or your color palette — your face. The AI uses your photos to understand what you look like, then applies that to whatever someone prompts it to make.

SAG-AFTRA told members to opt out immediately. The Creative Artists Agency called the policy "completely unacceptable." Privacy advocates who have watched this pattern for years aren't surprised — but they're alarmed by how direct it is.

If you have a public account and you've been posting because you want an audience, that's a choice you made consciously. Muse Image is a different choice — one made for you. What "public" meant when you created your account is not what "public" means now.

The opt-out is under Instagram settings → Privacy → AI tools. It takes two minutes. It doesn't make your account private — it removes your photos from being used as reference material without your knowledge.

### LinkedIn (~800 characters, max 1,800)

The coverage of Meta's Muse Image focused on SAG-AFTRA and celebrities. That's understandable — they were the loudest voices and the most obviously affected.

But the feature didn't have a follower minimum.

If you have a public Instagram — 200 followers, a food account, a hobby page — your photos were in scope. Muse Image doesn't distinguish between public figures and private individuals who chose to post publicly. A public account is a valid reference, full stop.

The second thing worth understanding: the coverage made it sound like a consent violation that happened to famous people. It isn't. It happened to anyone with a public account when the feature launched. That's a much larger group, and most of them never got a notification.

The most important shift in this story is what "public" now means. Before Muse Image, posting publicly meant people could see your photos. After Muse Image, it means your photos can be used as inputs to generate new content featuring your likeness. Those are not the same thing, and no one asked whether that shift was acceptable.
